# 2026 Australian Grand Prix

Formula 1's biggest regulation reset in years arrived in Melbourne, with
new power-unit rules mandating a roughly 50-50 split between battery and
internal-combustion power, alongside new active-aerodynamics rules.
Mercedes immediately looked like the team best prepared for it: George
Russell won comfortably from teammate Kimi Antonelli, with Ferrari's
Charles Leclerc third and Lewis Hamilton fourth in his second season at
the team. Defending champion Lando Norris, in the McLaren that had
dominated 2025, could only manage fifth.

The new power units rewarded careful energy management over outright
one-lap pace, and Russell and Leclerc spent much of the race swapping
the lead as each driver's battery deployment ebbed and flowed, producing
a more tactical, less spectacle-driven race than the regulations'
supporters had hoped for. It immediately established Mercedes as the
championship favorite heading into the new era.

The session was also brutal for Red Bull. Max Verstappen crashed during
qualifying and was forced to start from twentieth, recovering only to
sixth in the race — an early sign of the RB22's struggles that would
persist for most of the season's first half. Oscar Piastri, McLaren's
2025 championship runner-up, retired before the race even started, a
rough beginning to his title defense alongside Norris.
