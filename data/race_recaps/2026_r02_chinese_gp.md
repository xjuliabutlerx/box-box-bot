# 2026 Chinese Grand Prix

Kimi Antonelli took his first win of 2026 in Shanghai, starting from
pole and leading a Mercedes 1-2 ahead of teammate Russell. At nineteen
years old and in just his second F1 season, it was the first result that
made clear Antonelli wasn't simply benefiting from a strong car, but was
capable of leading a Mercedes team effort outright — a marked contrast
to a rookie 2025 campaign in which Russell had consistently had the
better of him.

Behind the Mercedes pair, Lewis Hamilton finished third for Ferrari, his
first podium since joining the team — a result he later called some of
the best racing he'd experienced in Formula 1, crediting the SF-26's
new-regulation package for suiting his driving style far better than his
difficult first season in red had.

Red Bull's weekend, by contrast, was a disaster. Max Verstappen retired
from the race with an engine-related failure after starting eighth, and
the team publicly admitted to "significant shortcomings" in the RB22
following the struggles of both Verstappen and teammate Isack Hadjar.
Two rounds into the new regulations, the shape of the 2026 season was
already becoming clear: Mercedes and Ferrari adapting well, Red Bull
fighting a car that wasn't just off the pace but structurally
unreliable.
