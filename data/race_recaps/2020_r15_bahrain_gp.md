# 2020 Bahrain Grand Prix

Lewis Hamilton won from pole in Bahrain, but the race is remembered above all for Romain Grosjean's horrifying first-lap crash, in which his Haas split apart on impact with the barriers and burst into flames. Grosjean climbed out of the wreckage himself less than thirty seconds later with only minor burns to his hands - an escape widely credited to the halo cockpit-protection device, introduced just two years earlier over significant resistance from parts of the paddock, which was seen to have taken the brunt of the impact as the car tore through the barrier.

The race was red-flagged for over an hour while the barrier was repaired and medical teams attended to the scene, before restarting with the remaining cars. Hamilton went on to win comfortably once racing resumed, but the result itself was overshadowed entirely by relief that Grosjean had survived a crash of a severity rarely seen in modern Formula 1, and by immediate, renewed conversation throughout the sport about the safety improvements of the preceding decade.

Grosjean did not race again that season, missing the following round while he recovered, but the crash became one of the defining moments in the case for the halo's necessity - a device that had been controversial enough at its introduction that some drivers had publicly criticized its appearance, and whose value was demonstrated at Bahrain in the starkest possible terms.
