# 2026 Monaco Grand Prix

Kimi Antonelli won from pole at Monaco to make it five consecutive victories dating back to the Chinese Grand Prix — an extraordinary run for a driver in just his second F1 season, and one that put him in a near-unassailable position atop the drivers' championship heading into the summer. Lewis Hamilton was second for Ferrari, with Red Bull's Isack Hadjar a surprise third.

The race around him was carnage. Seven cars failed to finish on a weekend that reinforced Monaco's reputation as the calendar's most unforgiving circuit for even a small mistake, with two separate safety car periods. The most costly retirement belonged to Max Verstappen, who crashed out on the opening lap from second on the grid — yet another instance of the RB22 "biting back" at the limit, continuing the reliability-and-driveability narrative that had dogged Red Bull's season from the opener onward. Charles Leclerc also failed to finish, retiring with two-thirds of the race complete.

Antonelli's streak would finally end the following round in Barcelona, but for five straight races, Mercedes' sophomore driver had simply not been beaten.
