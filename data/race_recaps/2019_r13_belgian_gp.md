# 2019 Belgian Grand Prix

Charles Leclerc won his first Formula 1 race at Spa-Francorchamps, converting pole position into a lights-to-flag victory ahead of Lewis Hamilton and his own teammate Sebastian Vettel, in a result that finally delivered on the potential Ferrari had signed him for. It was an emotionally heavy weekend for the sport: Formula 2 driver Anthoine Hubert, a close friend of Leclerc's from their junior-category days together, had died in a crash during Saturday's F2 race at the same circuit, and Leclerc dedicated the win to him immediately after crossing the line.

The victory was also a significant result in the broader championship picture. Ferrari had struggled for much of the season to convert raw pace into results, and Leclerc's win - backed up by strong straight-line speed through Spa's long Kemmel Straight - suggested the team had found genuine competitiveness against Mercedes heading into the final third of the year, even with the drivers' title already realistically out of reach.

For Leclerc personally, Spa marked the start of a run of consecutive victories that would continue at the very next round in Monza, establishing him as a genuine race-winning talent in just his first season at Ferrari and setting the tone for his position within the team for years afterward.
