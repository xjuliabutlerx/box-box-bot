# 2017 Mexican Grand Prix

Max Verstappen won in Mexico, but the race that mattered for the championship happened behind him: Lewis Hamilton clinched his fourth world title with two rounds still remaining, after a first-corner incident involving himself, Vettel, and Verstappen left Hamilton with front-wing damage early on. Vettel, who had started from pole, made contact with the back of Verstappen's car at the first corner, picking up his own damage in the process - though unlike Hamilton, he recovered well enough across the rest of the race to salvage fourth place by the finish.

Hamilton's own recovery drive was largely about damage limitation rather than victory-chasing: with his front wing compromised, he pitted for repairs and worked back through the field, content to finish well back of the podium so long as he out-scored Vettel by enough to make the title mathematically safe. By the checkered flag the numbers were beyond doubt, and Hamilton's fourth championship was confirmed before the podium ceremony even began.

The win itself went to Verstappen, continuing a strong late-season run that had also included victory at the previous Malaysian Grand Prix, and reinforcing Red Bull's position as a clear-if-distant third force behind Mercedes and Ferrari heading into 2018. For Ferrari, Mexico closed the book on a championship challenge that had looked genuinely competitive for much of the year before late-season reliability problems and this first-lap incident ended it for good.
