# 2016 Spanish Grand Prix

Max Verstappen won on his debut for Red Bull, at eighteen years and 228 days old, becoming the youngest Grand Prix winner in Formula 1 history. Red Bull had swapped him with Daniil Kvyat just four races into the season, promoting him from Toro Rosso - a promotion that looked like a huge gamble until Verstappen inherited the lead when the two Mercedes drivers, Lewis Hamilton and Nico Rosberg, collided with each other on the opening lap and both retired on the spot.

The Hamilton-Rosberg collision was the real story of the weekend beyond Verstappen's stunning result. Rosberg, defending the championship lead, moved to cover Hamilton's run down the inside at Turn 4; Hamilton went for a gap that closed, and the two title rivals made contact and were both out of the race instantly - a double-DNF for the dominant team that handed the entire podium to the chasing pack. It was the most serious flashpoint yet in the increasingly tense rivalry between the two Mercedes teammates, who had already clashed at the previous year's Belgian Grand Prix.

For Verstappen, Spain was the beginning of a partnership with Red Bull that would define the next decade of the sport. For Mercedes, the incident was a reminder that their intra-team rivalry carried real risk of costing the team results outright - a tension that would keep resurfacing as Hamilton and Rosberg fought for the title all the way to the season finale in Abu Dhabi.
