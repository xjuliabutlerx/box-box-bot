# 2018 United States Grand Prix

Kimi Räikkönen won in Austin, ending a winless streak stretching back over five years and becoming, at the time, the first driver other than Hamilton, Vettel, Rosberg, or a Red Bull driver to win a race since 2013. Max Verstappen, recovering brilliantly from eighteenth on the grid after a qualifying setback, took second, while Lewis Hamilton finished third - a result that was more than enough for Hamilton to secure his fifth world championship, since his closest rival Sebastian Vettel finished only fourth.

Räikkönen's win was a popular one within the paddock: the veteran Finn, in what was already known to be his final season with Ferrari before a move to Sauber for 2019, delivered a smooth, largely trouble-free race while his younger teammate Vettel's title hopes effectively ended for good. It stood as a reminder that Räikkönen, often overshadowed by teammates chasing championships, could still win outright when circumstances lined up.

For Hamilton, the title was his fifth, tying him for outright second on the all-time list at the time and continuing Mercedes' run of championship dominance through the V6 hybrid era. The Austin result also closed the book on Ferrari's championship challenge for the year - a campaign that had looked competitive through the first half of the season before a string of races, including the German Grand Prix crash, gradually eroded Vettel's advantage.
