# Kuala Lumpur Circuit

Note: this season's calendar lists the round held here under the "Bahrain Grand Prix" name, but the event actually runs at this Kuala Lumpur venue rather than Bahrain's own circuit - worth flagging so the name and the actual track don't get confused.

A wide, technical permanent circuit built for tropical conditions, with a long back straight, a demanding hairpin complex, and a layout wide enough to genuinely support side-by-side racing - a deliberate design choice that has historically made it one of the better circuits on the calendar for close, competitive racing. Kuala Lumpur's tropical climate brings intense heat, high humidity, and a real chance of sudden, heavy afternoon rain, both of which are major factors in car and tire management.

The long straight is a strong overtaking zone, and the wide track surface supports passing attempts elsewhere on the lap too. Heat and an abrasive track surface tend to push tire degradation higher than average, frequently favoring a two-stop strategy, and the ever-present chance of a tropical downpour means intermediate and wet-tire strategy calls are a real possibility even on a forecast-dry weekend.
