# Circuit de Spa-Francorchamps

At just over 7 km, Spa is the longest circuit on the calendar and one of the most revered, cut through the Ardennes forest with dramatic elevation changes - most famously Eau Rouge/Raidillon, a blind, uphill compression corner taken flat-out that remains one of the most iconic sequences in motorsport. The track's length and elevation mean weather can vary dramatically from one part of the lap to another, and Spa is notorious for localized rain that hits only part of the circuit.

The long Kemmel Straight after Eau Rouge is one of the best overtaking zones in Formula 1, giving cars a genuine run at the car ahead into Les Combes. Tire degradation is moderate, and strategy is usually built around a single stop, but Spa's unpredictable weather means teams often have to be ready to gamble on intermediate or wet tires at a moment's notice - more than almost anywhere else on the calendar.
