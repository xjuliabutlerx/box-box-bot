# Circuit de Monaco

Monaco is the tightest, slowest circuit on the calendar - narrow public roads through the streets of Monte Carlo, barriers on both sides for almost the entire 3.34 km lap, and barely enough width in places for two cars side by side. It's the ultimate test of precision: a driver's margin for error is measured in centimeters, and the tunnel, the harbor-front chicane, and the Fairmont Hairpin are among the most recognizable corners in motorsport.

Overtaking on track is nearly impossible - Monaco is famous for being won on Saturday, since qualifying position essentially determines the race result barring a safety car or strategy gamble. That makes track position the single most important factor in the entire weekend, and teams will often prioritize pure qualifying pace over race setup. It's traditionally a one-stop race, but the tight confines mean a single incident can trigger a safety car or red flag that completely reshuffles the field - Monaco has a well-earned reputation as one of the most unpredictable races precisely because it looks, on paper, like it should be the most processional.
