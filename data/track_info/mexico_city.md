# Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez

Mexico City sits at over 2,200 meters of altitude, giving the thin air a major effect on car performance: less aerodynamic drag but also less downforce and less cooling for the power unit and brakes, forcing teams into unusual aerodynamic setups compared to sea-level circuits. The lap combines a very long back straight with a distinctive final stadium section, run through a baseball-stadium-turned-corner complex packed with fans.

The long straight into Turn 1 is the primary overtaking zone, often producing dramatic passing (and equally dramatic lock-ups, given reduced downforce makes braking harder to judge). Because the thin air reduces cooling, brake and power unit management are bigger factors here than at most circuits, and tire degradation tends to be lower than the layout alone would suggest, often supporting a one-stop strategy.
