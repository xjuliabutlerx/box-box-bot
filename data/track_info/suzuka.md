# Suzuka International Racing Course

Suzuka is one of the most respected circuits on the calendar: a rare figure-eight layout (the back straight crosses over the front section via a flyover) combining the high-speed Esses, the long, committed 130R corner, and a stadium-like final sector, all taken at speeds that punish any driver lacking confidence in the car. It's widely regarded as one of the purest tests of driver skill in Formula 1.

Overtaking is genuinely difficult - most of the lap is flowing, high-commitment corners rather than hard-braking zones, so track position from qualifying matters enormously. The main passing chance is into the chicane before the start-finish straight. Tire degradation is moderate to high through the fast corners, and a one-stop is typical in dry conditions, though Suzuka's autumn weather (when it's held later in the season) can bring rain that scrambles strategy entirely.
