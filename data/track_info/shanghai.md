# Shanghai International Circuit

Shanghai's 5.45 km layout is built around a distinctive long, decreasing-radius Turn 1-2-3 complex (nicknamed for its shape, spiraling tighter as it goes) that rewards a car with strong front-end grip, followed by one of the longest back straights on the calendar into a hard-braking Turn 14. That combination of a technical opening sector and a flat-out back straight makes the track a genuine test of a car's all-round balance.

The long straight makes DRS-assisted overtaking into Turn 14 one of the most reliable passing zones on the calendar, so races here are rarely processional. Abrasive asphalt tends to punish tires more than most circuits, frequently pushing teams toward a two-stop strategy, and the track's history of hosting sprint weekends adds an extra layer of strategic complexity with a compressed on-track schedule.
